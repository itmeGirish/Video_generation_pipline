# Content Quality Gate

Run AFTER format passes. This gate asks: **is this script worth watching?**
Format validation catches parser errors. This catches boring videos.

## The 12 tests

A script must pass all 12 to proceed to render. Each test is PASS/FAIL with a reason.
Tests 1–6 judge whether it's worth watching; 7–11 judge whether it reads as fresh,
human, specific, real, and honestly critiqued; 12 judges whether it pulls the
psychological levers that actually make viewers watch and share.

---

## Test 1 — Hook strength (first 15 seconds)

The cold open must do one of these in the first 2-3 sentences:
- State a surprising or counterintuitive fact
- Pose a question the viewer cannot answer but wants to
- Show a contradiction that demands resolution
- Make a bold claim that challenges common belief

**PASS:** A viewer who lands on this video in the first 5 seconds has a reason to stay.
**FAIL:** Opens with background, definitions, or "in this video I'll show you..."

| FAIL pattern | PASS pattern |
|---|---|
| "Today we're going to talk about [topic]." | a surprising fact that contradicts what the viewer assumes |
| "Let me explain how [thing] works." | a bold claim that challenges common belief, stated flat |

(Patterns are illustrative — fill with your real, sourced hook from rule 00.)

---

## Test 2 — Unique angle

The script must say something the top 5 videos on this topic do NOT say.

Ask: search the topic on YouTube. Would this script already be in the top 5? If yes,
what makes THIS different?

**PASS:** Has a specific angle, contrarian take, or synthesis not found elsewhere.
**FAIL:** Covers exactly what every other video on the topic covers.

| FAIL angle | PASS angle |
|---|---|
| "What is [topic]?" (same as a hundred other videos) | "Why [topic] is quietly being replaced — and what's winning" |
| a generic listicle | a specific first-person result or a contrarian reframe |

---

## Test 3 — Viewer benefit (does it give real value?)

The core question: **does this video actually GIVE the viewer something?** If they finish
and gained nothing they couldn't get elsewhere in 10 seconds, it has no value — and no
value means no watch time, no shares, no return viewers.

Value comes in at least one of these forms. Name which one(s) this script delivers:
- **A skill / how-to** — they can now DO something they couldn't before
- **A decision** — they can now CHOOSE between options with a clear rule
- **A mental model / reframe** — they now UNDERSTAND something in a way that sticks
- **A genuinely surprising fact** — they KNOW something worth knowing and repeating
- **Time saved** — it answers, in minutes, something that would take them hours
- **Entertainment with a point** — even if the value is enjoyment, there's a takeaway

**PASS:** the viewer can finish and name the specific thing they got — "now I can X" /
"now I understand why Y" / "I didn't know Z." The value is real and specific.
**FAIL:** pure information dump, restates what they already knew, or value so vague the
viewer couldn't say what they gained ("learned about [topic]" is not value).

The test: complete this sentence honestly — *"A viewer finishes this and can now ______."*
If you can't fill the blank with something specific and real, the script gives no value.

Fix: add a concrete takeaway — a rule, a framework, a surprising fact, an actionable step
— and make the final scene deliver it explicitly (rule 01 verdict).

---

## Test 4 — Evidence quality

Every major claim must be backed by something real:
- A statistic with a source
- A named person's quote
- A demonstrable example
- A reproducible test or benchmark

**PASS:** Claims are backed by research (rule 00) — real numbers, real sources.
**FAIL:** Claims are assertions with no evidence ("everyone knows," "it's obvious").

---

## Test 5 — Tension and resolution

The script must create and resolve tension — the engine of retention.

**PASS:** Opens a loop (question, mystery, conflict), keeps it open, resolves it at the end.
**FAIL:** Flat delivery — no question raised, no payoff promised, no resolution.

---

## Test 6 — The "so what" test

For every scene, ask: "so what?" If a scene doesn't change the viewer's understanding or
move the argument forward, it fails.

**PASS:** Every scene earns its place — cut any scene and the video is worse.
**FAIL:** Scenes that could be cut with no loss — filler, repetition, tangents.

---

## Test 7 — Content freshness

Is the material current and not stale? Especially for AI/tech, facts rot in weeks.

- The hook fact, numbers, and examples are recent (current year/quarter where it matters)
- Nothing references a version, price, or benchmark that has since been superseded
- The angle reacts to what's happening NOW, not a debate that's already settled
- Verified against the research (rule 00), not written from stale memory

**PASS:** the facts are current and would still be true today; nothing reads dated.
**FAIL:** stale numbers, outdated comparisons, or a take that the news has moved past.

Fix: re-run the relevant searches (rule 00), refresh the figures, re-aim the angle.

---

## Test 8 — Human, not AI

Does it read like a person wrote it, or like generated filler? AI tells:

- Robotic filler words: "delve," "tapestry," "moreover," "furthermore," "in conclusion,"
  "it's important to note," "in today's fast-paced world," "unlock the power of"
- Repeated transition openers — the same frame opening multiple scenes ("Here's the…,"
  "Here's what's strange…," "Here's the part nobody…," "Now here's…"). Scan all scene
  openers together; if a template opens more than one scene, it reads generated (rule 02).
- Uniform sentence length and even paragraph blocks — no rhythm
- Surface-level, no real opinion, no specific lived detail
- Grammatically perfect but soulless — no idioms, no edge, no voice
- Repeated transition phrases and templated structure

**PASS:** specific voice, an opinion, varied rhythm, concrete detail — clearly human-shaped.
**FAIL:** reads as generated — filler vocabulary, no stance, flat uniform cadence.

Run the **banned-word scan** (rule 02 list): scan the narration for filler ("delve,"
"in today's world," "it's important to note," etc.) and for vague quantifiers ("a lot,"
"many," "significant" with no number after). Any hit = FAIL until fixed.

Fix: rewrite in spoken voice (rule 02), strip every banned word, add a real opinion and
a concrete specific per scene. (Pairs with rule 05b Q1 and the rule 02 specificity pass.)

## Test 9 — Not generic

Could this script be about a hundred other topics with a find-and-replace? If so, it's
generic.

- Specific to THIS topic — names, numbers, and examples only this video has
- Not a definition/Wikipedia summary anyone could write without research
- Has a stance, not a balanced "on the one hand / on the other hand" non-position

**PASS:** unmistakably about this specific topic, with a specific point of view.
**FAIL:** swap the topic word and it still works — that's generic filler.

The test, line by line: pick a sentence and swap the topic word. If it still reads fine,
that line is generic — it needs a concrete specific only this topic has (rule 02).

Fix: bring in the specific researched facts (rule 00) and the unique angle (Test 2).

### Test 9b — Not predictable

Generic is "could be any topic." Predictable is "I can guess the next sentence." Both lose
the viewer — predictability is boredom in advance. If the audience can finish your sentence,
they have no reason to keep listening.

Scan for predictability:
- **Obvious conclusions** — the line the viewer already expected ("…and so it grew.").
- **Telegraphed payoffs** — the reveal is guessable from the setup; the surprise isn't.
- **Repeated sentence structures** — the same rhythm/shape several lines in a row.
- **Stock transitions** — "Let's dive in," "But first," "Now, you might be wondering."

**The test:** cover the second half of each key sentence and predict it. If your guess is
right, rewrite — invert it, withhold it, or replace the obvious word with the surprising
true one (the counterintuitive fact from research, rule 00). A line the viewer can predict
is a line they don't need to watch for.

**PASS:** the next beat is hard to predict — each turn earns attention.
**FAIL:** the script is guessable — obvious conclusions, telegraphed reveals, repeated shapes.

## Test 10 — Not over-polished

Counterintuitive but real: content that's TOO polished reads sterile, corporate, and
fake. Audiences trust a human voice over a flawless press release.

- Has personality, a little roughness, a real aside or opinion — not buffed smooth
- Doesn't sound like marketing copy or a brand statement
- Keeps spontaneity: a joke, a hot take, an honest "this part is annoying"
- Confident, not hedged into blandness

**PASS:** clear and tight, but still sounds like a real person with a point of view.
**FAIL:** so smooth it's sterile — impersonal, corporate, no human texture.

Fix: loosen the voice, add an honest opinion or aside, cut marketing-speak. Polished
enough to be clear, human enough to be trusted.

---

## Test 11 — The critique pass

Before approving, deliberately ATTACK the script — find its weakest points. Don't praise
it; critique it like a harsh reviewer who wants it to fail.

Ask, bluntly:
- What's the weakest scene, and why would a viewer leave there?
- Which claim is least convincing or least supported?
- Where does it drag, repeat, or state the obvious?
- What would a skeptic in the comments call out?
- If this got 100 views and no shares, what's the most likely reason?

Write the 2–3 harshest, most honest criticisms — then confirm each is fixed or accepted.

**PASS:** the script survives an honest harsh critique; weak points are fixed.
**FAIL:** the critique surfaces a real problem that hasn't been addressed.

This is a thinking pass, not a checkbox — be genuinely critical, not reassuring.

## Test 12 — Viewer psychology

Content works because it pulls real psychological levers. A script that ignores how
viewers' brains actually work will be technically fine and still get ignored. Check the
script triggers at least the first three, and ideally a sharing driver too.

**1. Curiosity gap (Zeigarnik effect).** The brain craves closure on an open question —
an unfinished loop is remembered and pulls the viewer forward.
- PASS: a clear gap is opened early and held; the viewer NEEDS the answer.
- FAIL: everything is stated up front; nothing is left open to resolve.

**2. Anticipation / dopamine.** Reward chemistry fires in *anticipation*, not just at
payoff — teasers and "what's coming" promises keep viewers watching.
- PASS: the script promises a payoff ahead and makes the viewer want to reach it.
- FAIL: no forward promise; no reason to expect something better is coming.

**3. High-arousal emotion.** Awe, surprise, excitement, even mild anger drive watching
and sharing far more than flat, low-arousal information.
- PASS: at least one genuine high-arousal moment (a surprise, an "whoa," a stake).
- FAIL: emotionally flat — true and complete, but nothing is *felt*.

**4. Social currency / identity (why they'd SHARE).** People share what makes them look
smart, in-the-know, or expresses who they are.
- PASS: there's a fact or reframe a viewer would share to look informed, or that fits
  an identity ("people like me care about this").
- FAIL: nothing here makes the viewer look good by passing it on.

**5. Practical value / relatability.** Useful, relatable content gets shared because it
helps someone — and viewers stay for content that mirrors their own situation.
- PASS: a clear "this is useful to me / this is about my situation" hit.
- FAIL: abstract and detached — nothing the viewer can use or see themselves in.

**PASS overall:** triggers curiosity + anticipation + emotion, and gives at least one
reason to share. **FAIL:** emotionally flat, no open loop, nothing shareable.

Fix: open a curiosity gap and promise a payoff (rule 01 loops / rule 05a), add the
high-arousal beat from research (rule 00), and plant one share-worthy line (rule 05a).

## A note on animation / visual quality

This gate judges the **written content**. Whether the animation makes sense, looks good,
and is buildable is judged separately by the **animation validator (rule 03b)** —
clarity (does the motion mean something), show-don't-tell (not a text slide), richness,
and "can Remotion build it." Run both: 06 for the words, 03b for the visuals. A script is
only ready when the content AND the animation pass.

## Output format

```
CONTENT QUALITY GATE — <name>

Test 1  Hook:          PASS — the cold open states the surprising fact up front
Test 2  Angle:         PASS — a contrarian take not covered by the top videos
Test 3  Value:         PASS — viewer leaves able to "now choose X using rule Y"
Test 4  Evidence:      WARN — one scene's stat needs a source
Test 5  Tension:       PASS — opens the central question, resolves it at the end
Test 6  So-what:       PASS — every scene advances the argument
Test 7  Freshness:     PASS — figures are current, nothing dated
Test 8  Human-not-AI:  WARN — Scene 3 has filler ("it's important to note")
Test 9  Not-generic:   PASS — specific to this topic, has a stance
Test 10 Not-polished:  PASS — clear but still has a human voice
Test 11 Critique:      PASS — weakest scene (S4) tightened after critique
Test 12 Psychology:    PASS — curiosity gap + anticipation + a shareable reframe

VERDICT: READY (fix the Test 4 + Test 8 warnings first)
```

## Guidelines

### Always
- Run all 12 tests on the full script before final approval
- Confirm the script gives real, specific value (Test 3) — "a viewer finishes and can now ___"
- Check viewer psychology (Test 12) — curiosity gap, anticipation, emotion, a reason to share
- Run the critique pass (Test 11) honestly — attack the script, don't reassure it
- Verify freshness against the research (rule 00), not from memory
- Strip AI-filler vocabulary; keep a real human voice and a stance
- Be honest — a script that fails these tests will underperform no matter how good the production
- Fix FAILs before render — they are not optional
- Run the animation validator (rule 03b) too — this gate covers words, not visuals

### Never
- Skip the content gate because the format passed
- Approve a script that fails the hook test — the hook determines most of retention
- Ship a script that gives the viewer no real takeaway ("learned about [topic]" is not value)
- Ship content that reads AI-generated, generic, stale, or sterile-over-polished
- Proceed with unresolved FAILs
